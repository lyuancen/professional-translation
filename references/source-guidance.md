# Source selection for terminology questions

Use these as starting points, then cite the *specific page or document* actually read. Verify applicable model, market, jurisdiction, and version. A URL listed here is not evidence that a particular term has been verified.

| Context | First places to check |
| --- | --- |
| Commercial vehicle systems and specifications | Relevant OEM's English product page or technical brochure (SINOTRUK, Volvo Trucks, Scania, Mercedes-Benz Trucks, MAN, DAF, Iveco), then the component supplier (ZF, Bosch, Cummins, SKF) |
| Vehicle regulatory terminology | [UNECE vehicle regulations](https://unece.org/transport/vehicle-regulations), [European Commission automotive](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/sectors/automotive-industry_en), applicable published ISO/SAE material |
| Corporate governance and market disclosure | [HKEXnews](https://www.hkexnews.hk/), relevant issuer's English annual report/circular/announcement, [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/) for US filings |
| International trade and contracts | [ICC Incoterms](https://iccwbo.org/business-solutions/incoterms-rules/), [UNCITRAL](https://uncitral.un.org/), the governing jurisdiction's legislation/regulator and relevant official model text |

For a Word entry with a clear applicable usage note, use that entry without routine web searching. Check externally when an unfamiliar term, a material ambiguity, a current rule/name, or a possible error could change the translation. Prefer original English usage over third-party bilingual lists. A single occurrence is weak evidence; compare scope and context. When a source conflicts materially with the Word, show the conflict and recommended handling rather than silently overwriting the Word.
